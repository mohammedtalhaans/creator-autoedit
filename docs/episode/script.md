# Episode script

**Working title:** I’m Building a Browser Editor for Creators

**Read:** relaxed, specific, curious. The time ranges include screen demonstrations and source-video playback; they are guides rather than fixed narration durations. Aim for 3–4 minutes. Record one sentence at a time if that is easier.

**Prerequisite:** follow the claim check in [README.md](README.md). Bracketed directions are not spoken. The current Windows browser evidence proves real with-audio/no-audio export and playback, while model inference and physical-device support remain unverified. Include the captions section only when this recording's caption workflow completes; if you enter the transcript yourself, say “I added these captions.” Do not call a fixture transcript or a failed model attempt automatic generation.

## Main episode

### 0:00–0:15 — Show the problem and result

[Open on 3 seconds of the raw clip, including a noticeable pause. Hard cut to the same sentence in the exported edit. Label each clip clearly. Return to camera.]

“That gap is the kind of thing I keep cutting out of talking videos. So I’m building a little browser editor to make that first pass easier. This is Creator AutoEdit, and today I’m taking one raw clip all the way to a saved video.”

### 0:15–0:45 — What the app is for

[Show the home screen, then choose your own source file.]

“The idea is pretty focused: one person talking, one video, and a few decisions. Where should the pauses go? Are the captions right? And does the shot work vertically?

“I want the routine part of editing to be easier, while still letting me decide what the finished clip feels like. So I’m using a deliberately awkward take here. It has a couple of long gaps, and I’ve left enough room around me to change the crop.”

### 0:45–1:25 — Make the pacing decision visible

[Show the pause list or timeline. Play the same moment before and after cutting. Switch pacing once if available. Restore one pause and play it again.]

“First, the pauses. The app identifies quiet sections so I can tighten the pacing. Listen to this bit.”

[Let the viewer hear the comparison; pause narration.]

“That moves better. But I actually want a little space before this next thought, so I’m keeping this pause. That control matters. A gap might be dead air, or it might be part of how you tell the story.

“This is also pause cutting, not a promise to understand every ‘um’ or every sentence I should remove. I still listen to the edit.”

### 1:25–2:05 — Captions that can be corrected (only if verified for this recording)

[Show real captions from the demonstrated workflow. Correct a word or make an intentional wording adjustment. Choose one readable style. Play a complete phrase.]

“Next are the captions. I review them before I worry about how they look. Names, punctuation, and short words are the things I check carefully.

“I can change the text here, then choose a style that is easy to read over the footage. I’m keeping this one simple. The words should help you follow what I’m saying without covering the whole shot.

“And I check a full phrase in playback, because a good-looking still frame doesn’t tell me whether the timing feels right.”

### 2:05–2:35 — Frame the shot

[Change to vertical framing, adjust the crop, and show the moving preview.]

“Now I’m making the frame vertical. I’m checking where my face lands, whether the captions have enough space, and what happens when I move.

“This is why I left some room in the original recording. A crop can move the shot around, but it can’t bring back something I recorded outside the frame.”

### 2:35–3:05 — Prove the output

[Export the actual edit. If the wait is shortened, show “Export sped up” or “Time jump.” Save the file and play it in a separate video player.]

“Now for the part that makes this an editor: saving the result. I’m exporting this take, downloading the file, and opening that file separately.”

[Play 5–8 seconds of the downloaded result with its own audio.]

“The cuts, the captions, and the framing need to work in that file, not just inside the preview. I also listen around the cuts and check that the voice stays in time with the picture.”

### 3:05–3:35 — Close with the real status

[Return to camera, with the finished video beside you if convenient.]

“That’s the workflow I’m trying to make useful: a raw take, a few deliberate choices, and a video I can actually use.

“There’s still plenty to test with different recordings and devices. For this episode, the important thing was getting this whole path working and checking the result.

“If you make talking videos, which part takes you the longest: cutting the pauses, fixing the captions, or getting the framing right? Tell me below. That’s useful feedback for what I work on next.”

[Only after the site is deployed and checked, add: “You can try this build through the link in the description.” Otherwise end without a live-demo claim.]

## 45–60-second cut

**Target:** approximately 120 words, plus 3–5 seconds of clip playback. Crop the screen capture around the active control; do not squeeze the entire desktop interface into a phone frame.

**0:00–0:05 — Raw gap → edited line.**

“I’m building a browser editor for the awkward gaps in talking videos.”

**0:05–0:15 — Choose file → pause controls.**

“This is Creator AutoEdit. I start with one take, tighten the pauses, and keep the ones that actually help the delivery.”

**0:15–0:29 — Restore a pause → correct a caption (only if available in this recording).**

“Then I review the captions. I want the words to be right before I make them look good. Everything here should still be something I can change.”

**0:29–0:40 — Vertical crop → export.**

“I adjust the vertical frame, export it, and open the downloaded file to check the actual result.”

**0:40–0:49 — Play finished result; leave room for its audio.**

“Here’s the same take after that first pass.”

**0:49–0:60 — Camera or end card.**

“What would save you more time: better cuts, better captions, or better framing? I’m building this around that workflow.”

**On-screen text:** “ONE TAKE → FIRST EDIT” at the opening; “REVIEW THE RESULT” over the downloaded-video proof; “CUTS / CAPTIONS / FRAMING?” at the end.
